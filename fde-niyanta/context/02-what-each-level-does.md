# 2. What each level does

Each layer of the grid asks a different question, works on a different clock, and needs a different screen. If you give Kartik and Mr. Kamath the same screen with a different filter, one of them is looking at the wrong question.

| Level | Question it answers | Works on | Clock | PDGMS module |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V5** | Are we reaching the goal? Did the number move? | The goal and its direction | Quarterly review, yearly goal | **Niyanta** (the goal and its levers) |
| **V4** | Are our programs together moving the strategy? | Several programs in one function | Monthly review, quarterly change of course | **Niyanta** (programs, directions, structural problems) |
| **V3** | Is the cross-department machine producing results at the rate we committed? | One program across many departments | Daily to quarterly | **Mimamsaka** |
| **V2** | Did my team deliver its work on time and at quality? | One department across many programs | Daily, weekly | **Trinethra**, Aavahana |
| **V1** | Did this piece of work get done? | One person, one task | Daily | **Aavahana** |

This assignment is about the top row and the second row. Ritika and Aditya work in Mimamsaka at V3. What they send up arrives in Niyanta.

## V5: the goal

- **Sets the goal.** At Anantha: EBITDA from ₹13.4 Cr to ₹24 Cr by end of FY28.
- **Sets direction.** "Keep 10% of the plant free for specialty work." Not "Imran, call this customer."
- **Allocates money, people and priority.**
- **Reviews whether the strategy is working**, and changes course when it is not.

Throughput at V5 is simple: did the number move?

## V4: the strategy for one function

- **Decides which programs exist** and approves them. Ritika's growth program and Aditya's delivery program were approved by Bhandari and Kamath.
- **Approves changes** a V3 asks for: more time, less scope, a different plan.
- **Resolves problems a program cannot solve itself**: money, another department, a policy.
- **Takes a V5 direction and turns it into choices**, or declines it for named cases with a reason.

Throughput at V4: are the programs reaching their targets, and together, are they moving the V5 goal?

## V3: across departments

The hard problems sit at the boundary between departments. If a problem lived inside one department, its head would fix it. A sample request that waits ten days is not sales' fault alone or the plant's alone: it waits in the gap between them. V3 is the first level that can see that gap, so it is where throughput is first measured honestly. Every department can hit its own targets while the company as a whole delivers nothing.

## How a goal travels down

```
V5  Kartik and Pranav   "EBITDA ₹13.4 Cr to ₹24 Cr by FY28"
 └ V4  Bhandari         "Win specification-led accounts in curtains and upholstery"
   └ V3  Ritika         Growth program: research, sampling, the trial with Hearthline
     └ V2  Regional lead  Assigns accounts, protects existing business
       └ V1  Harpreet     "Today I send Hearthline the trial plan"
```

Authority goes down. Accountability comes back up. From any day's work you should be able to walk up to the goal it serves, and from the goal down to the work.

## Where the levels push against each other

| Between | What happens |
|---|---|
| V1 and V2 | "I'm stuck." The supervisor unblocks it or escalates. |
| V2 and V3 | "If you take my people for your program, my daily output drops." |
| V3 and V4 | "We need two more weeks." V4 approves, or cuts scope instead. |
| V4 and V5 | "This direction does not work for these two accounts." V5 accepts the reason, or holds the direction and says why. |

These pushes are not failures. They are how the levels do their jobs. A V5 screen that hides them, or shows them as disobedience, makes the company worse.
