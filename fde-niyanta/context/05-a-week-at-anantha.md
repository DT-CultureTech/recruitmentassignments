# 5. A week at Anantha, without Niyanta

This is how the four owners work today. Niyanta replaces some of it. Knowing what it replaces tells you what each screen has to beat.

## How decisions happen now

- **Orders** are entered in the ERP. Each day the planning team exports open orders to a spreadsheet and decides what runs on which line.
- **Changes** to that plan travel by phone and WhatsApp. Nobody writes down why a line was changed or an order jumped the queue.
- **Sales** reviews tonnes against monthly targets on a weekly video call, region by region.
- **Plant and sales** sit together once a quarter.
- **Escalations** from Ritika and Aditya arrive as WhatsApp messages and emails. Some get answered in a day. Some never do.

Nothing is wrong with any one of these. The trouble is that each owner sees only their own part, and the cost of a decision lands on someone who was not in the room.

## Kartik, MD

**Looks at:** the monthly P&L from Finance, the tonnes dispatched, and the five large accounts he prices himself.

**Cannot see:** what a priority he sets does to everyone else's orders. On 30 September he marked a 12-tonne order for Velan urgent. It jumped six orders on two lines. Four of them will now miss their promised date. He has not seen that yet; Aditya raised it on 1 October (escalation `E03`).

**Wants from a screen:** the decisions only he can take, with what each one costs and who pays.

## Pranav, Executive Director

**Looks at:** whether the plant is ready for more demand, and how the sales team talks about specialty work.

**Cannot see:** whether the two wins he asked for are moving. He named the direction on 1 September; Bhandari named Silvara Home and Hearthline Living as the two. He hears about them only when he asks.

**Wants from a screen:** whether the plant can deliver what sales is about to promise, and where his two wins stand.

## Mr. Bhandari, Commercial GM

**Looks at:** tonnes by region against target, and his team's list of accounts.

**Cannot see:** how long the plant will take to answer a customer's brief, so he cannot promise a date. Two pursuits were lost while a sample or a reply was late.

**Did last month:** declined Kartik's direction of 4 August for two large traders, and wrote why. He has not heard back.

**Wants from a screen:** which pursuits need a push from him, what the plant can promise, and what the owners have decided that affects his team.

## Mr. Kamath, Plant Head

**Looks at:** yesterday's output by line, losses by reason, and the queue at texturising and twisting.

**Cannot see:** which orders matter more than others to the business, except when someone senior phones. He holds two lines (L09 and L10) for specialty under Kartik's direction, and three specialty orders on L09 are waiting for a polymer campaign that has not filled (escalation `E01`).

**Wants from a screen:** what to run next and why, what an override will cost before he accepts it, and which orders are going to miss their date.

## The Monday that Niyanta should change

Today, on a Monday morning, each of the four starts from a different report. By lunch, a priority has been phoned through, a trader has been offered a discount, and a line has been changed, each without the other three knowing. Niyanta is the place where those four mornings start from the same facts, each seen from that person's job.
